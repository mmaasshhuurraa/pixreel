# Online photo-to-video / slideshow makers and image-to-video converters: PixReel's web competitors

**Methodology caveat (read first).** All facts were accessed on **2026-11-05**. Direct page fetching was blocked in this research session (the network policy rejected kapwing.com, flexclip.com, veed.io, animoto.com, clideo.com and canva.com). So nearly every finding below comes from search-result snippets. Most of those snippets are **third-party 2026 reviews and pricing aggregators**, not vendor pages. Where the snippet did come from a vendor page (Clideo, ezgif, Convertio support, img2go, Clipchamp pricing, Kapwing help, Canva dev docs), it is marked **[official]**. Third-party prices often disagree, so ranges are given. Before publishing, re-check the numbers on the vendors' own pricing pages. Data I could not confirm is marked **unverified**.

## Q1. Product roster: free-tier limits, max resolution and pricing (USD)

### Takeaway
Almost every mainstream online maker uses the same free-tier pattern: a **watermark** plus a **720p cap** (Renderforest is lower at about 360p), with 1080p and 4K behind roughly $9–$30/month plans. The notable exceptions are **Clipchamp** (1080p, no watermark, free [official]), **Adobe Express** (no watermark on free video export, per reviews) and **Canva** (sources conflict). Simple converters (ezgif, Convertio, img2go) have no watermark but cap file size and daily use.

### Cited Findings

**Kapwing**
- Free: watermark on every export; **720p** cap; about **30 min of export per month**. Sources disagree on the per-export length limit (1 min vs 4 min) and on storage (2 GB upload / 3 projects vs 5 GB) — [reelscut.ai](https://reelscut.ai/en/alternatives/kapwing); [morphed.app](https://morphed.app/blog/kapwing-free-plan-limits); [socialrails](https://socialrails.com/blog/kapwing-pricing)
- Pro is about **$16/mo billed annually or $24/mo billed monthly**. Business is **$50/mo annually or $64/mo monthly**. Enterprise is custom. Paid plans reach 4K, though one aggregator says Pro is 1080p only — [socialrails](https://socialrails.com/blog/kapwing-pricing); [aisotools](https://aisotools.com/pricing/kapwing)
- **[official]** The help page gives the resolution limits as 720p SD on Free and 1080p HD on paid plans. This may be older than the 4K claim above — [Kapwing help: Upload](https://www.kapwing.com/help/upload/)

**FlexClip**

One review says the limit is 1 minute instead — [tooljunction](https://www.tooljunction.io/ai-tools/flexclip); [creatorstackclub](https://www.creatorstackclub.com/software/flexclip); [saascrmreview](https://saascrmreview.com/flexclip-review/)
- Plus is about **$11.99/mo annually** (some say $9.99) and gives 1080p with no watermark. Business is about **$19.99/mo annually** (one source says $29.99 monthly) and adds **4K** — [aisotools](https://aisotools.com/pricing/flexclip); [toolchase](https://toolchase.com/tool/flexclip/)

**VEED**

SaaSworthy is the outlier that says there is no free plan — [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/veed-io-pricing-2026); [dupple](https://dupple.com/pricing/veed); [saasworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/veed-io/pricing)

### Inferences

- Paid prices cluster around **$9–16/mo** (annual) for 1080p and **$20–30/mo** for 4K or business plans.
- The **slideshow-focused legacy tools** (Kizoa, Smilebox, Animoto, Biteable) are shrinking or pivoting: Biteable dropped its free tier, Smilebox has no verifiable 2026 pricing page, and Kizoa's data is stale.

### Gaps

## Q2. Supported input formats (HEIC, RAW, PSD, PDF, AVIF, animated GIF, video clips)

### Takeaway
Explicit format lists were found for only a few tools. **Kapwing accepts HEIC/HEIF [official]**. **Canva's developer docs list HEIC**, though older posts say it is unsupported. **ezgif images-to-video accepts only GIF, JPG and PNG (plus ZIP)**. No evidence was found that any web slideshow maker accepts camera RAW, PSD, EXR or JXL as slideshow input. This is a possible differentiator for PixReel's planned server API.

### Cited Findings
- Kapwing: the help page lists **.heic and .heif** among supported image types, alongside .mp4, .mov and other video formats. Kapwing also offers a separate HEIC→JPG tool — [Kapwing help](https://www.kapwing.com/help/upload/); [Kapwing HEIC to JPG](https://www.kapwing.com/tools/convert/heic-to-jpg)
- Canva: the developer SDK docs list **image/heic** as a supported upload type. A third-party page gives a HEIC limit of under 25 MB and at most 100 million pixels. An older blog says Canva won't open HEIC, so the sources conflict — [Canva dev docs](https://www.canva.dev/docs/apps/uploading-assets/); [Pttrns](https://www.pttrns.com/canva-file-types-and-restrictions/); [wildandfreetools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/canva-alternative-heic-to-jpg/)
- ezgif images-to-video: inputs are **GIF, JPG and PNG, or a ZIP of them**. AVIF, WebP and HEIC are not listed for this tool — [ezgif](https://ezgif.com/images-to-video)
- Open-source ffmpeg.wasm slideshow maker (aahan225): takes **JPG, PNG, WebP and BMP** — [GitHub aahan225/photo_slideshow_maker](https://github.com/aahan225/photo_slideshow_maker)
- All mainstream editors (Kapwing, VEED, Canva, Clipchamp, FlexClip, Clideo) accept **video clips** as timeline input. Kapwing's .mp4 and .mov support is [official] — [Kapwing help](https://www.kapwing.com/help/upload/)

### Inferences
- PixReel accepts AVIF, APNG, animated WebP, ICO and SVG directly in the browser, and its planned HEIC, RAW, PSD, PDF and EXR support goes further. That is probably broader input coverage than any web competitor here, but competitor lists could not be confirmed for most tools.

### Gaps
- No official input-format lists were found for VEED, FlexClip, Clideo, Animoto, Renderforest, InVideo, Adobe Express, Fotor, Clipchamp or Convertio for **HEIC, RAW, PSD, PDF or AVIF**. These need checking in each help center.
- Whether animated GIF frames play animated or appear as a still in slideshow makers: unverified for every product.

## Q3. Uploads, signup and privacy-first local tools

### Takeaway
All mainstream makers are **cloud editors that need an account**. ezgif and Convertio need **no account** but **do upload** files (ezgif deletes them after 1 h). The truly local, no-upload space consists of a few **small GitHub projects** built on **ffmpeg.wasm or WebCodecs**. No polished, commercial-grade browser-local slideshow product with transitions and multi-track audio was found, which is PixReel's niche.

### Cited Findings
- ezgif deletes uploaded files automatically 1 hour after upload. That means files do go to its server — [ezgif](https://ezgif.com/images-to-video)
- img2go: an account is optional for most tools, and a free account unlocks history and higher daily limits — [img2go](https://www.img2go.com/)
- Clipchamp: editable projects rely on OneDrive (cloud). Whether rendering happens locally or in the cloud was not stated in 2026 sources — [reelscut.ai](https://reelscut.ai/en/alternatives/clipchamp)
- **CINEMA SLIDE (tri-woods/slideshow-maker):** a browser photo→MP4 PWA that encodes locally through **WebCodecs** with "no backend to upload them to". Up to **150 photos**, several resolutions, optional music, works offline once loaded. No Product Hunt listing was found — [GitHub tri-woods/slideshow-maker](https://github.com/tri-woods/slideshow-maker)
- **photo_slideshow_maker (aahan225):** a client-side MP4 export via ffmpeg.wasm that loads the core from a CDN on first export. Static slides only, **no transitions or motion**. Large batches can run out of tab memory — [GitHub](https://github.com/aahan225/photo_slideshow_maker)
- **ffmpeg-web (dinoosauro):** a general ffmpeg.wasm UI with an image-to-video mode (codec, container, FPS, scaling). "Nothing is sent to a server" — [GitHub](https://github.com/dinoosauro/ffmpeg-web)
- **ffmpeg-webCLI (tejaswigowda):** a browser video editor on ffmpeg.wasm, "No uploads, no servers" — [GitHub](https://github.com/tejaswigowda/ffmpeg-webCLI)
- Products that look like competitors but are **not local**: Cinematic Slideshow Studio (cloud 4K export, mentioned on Product Hunt), Flixier (cloud), SuperMotion (needs image upload), and PicX Studio (local, but exports GIF only) — [cinematicslideshowstudio.com](https://cinematicslideshowstudio.com/); [Flixier](https://flixier.com/create/slideshow-maker-online); [SuperMotion](https://www.supermotion.co/tools/image-slideshow); [PicX Studio](https://picxstudio.com/tools/photo-slideshow)
- WebCodecs browser support is about 95.5% (2025, vendor blog claim) — [velocaption](https://velocaption.com/blog/webcodecs-browser-video-editing/)

### Inferences
- PixReel's "no upload, no signup, WebCodecs" positioning is directly contested only by hobby-grade open-source tools (CINEMA SLIDE is the closest). These lack PixReel's breadth: 13 transitions, many output containers, multi-track music with crossfade, EXIF sort and a RU/EN UI.
- For the mainstream makers, signup is required to export. This is widely known, but it was **not individually verified** in this session for each product.

### Gaps
- Signup-to-export rules were not confirmed per product (Clideo reportedly allows some use without signup, but this is unverified).
- No Product Hunt listings were found for privacy-first slideshow makers (searches returned nothing relevant).

## Q4. Editing features: transitions, Ken Burns, text, templates, AI, music

### Takeaway
Little per-product data on feature counts was found. The mainstream editors compete on **templates, stock media and AI** (subtitles, avatars, text-to-video). PixReel competes on core slideshow mechanics and audio mixing.

### Cited Findings
- VEED Pro adds AI tools: Clean Audio, Eye Contact, Magic Cut, AI Clips, subtitle translation, brand kit and AI avatars — [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/veed-io-pricing-2026)
- FlexClip free allows only 1 stock video or audio clip per project — [tooljunction](https://www.tooljunction.io/ai-tools/flexclip)
- InVideo's model is AI generation measured in AI minutes and credits — [flowith](https://flowith.io/blog/invideo-pricing-2026-free-vs-plus-vs-max/)
- Canva's AI video clips (Magic Media / Dream Lab) are capped at about 8 s per clip (June 2026). On free they are 4 s and silent — [fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/canva-video-pricing-2026); [creatorstackclub](https://www.creatorstackclub.com/software/canva-video-editor)
- The basic ffmpeg.wasm slideshow repo has no transitions or motion — [GitHub aahan225](https://github.com/aahan225/photo_slideshow_maker)

### Inferences
- Multi-track music with per-track volume and crossfade is unusual in simple slideshow makers. Most such tools are commonly understood to offer one background track. **Unverified** for each product.

### Gaps
- Transition counts, Ken Burns availability, multi-track audio and per-track volume could not be extracted from any official page in this session. This needs a manual pass over each product's help center.

## Q5. Russian-language UI and availability or payment from Russia

### Takeaway
**Canva has blocked access in Russia and stopped taking Russian payments.** Users report needing foreign cards or intermediaries. No data was found for the other products. Payment from Russia is presumably hard for all the US/EU SaaS tools, but this is **unverified**.

### Cited Findings
- Canva announced it was stopping work in Russia and blocking access indefinitely, and it stopped processing payments for the Russian region. Mir cards are not accepted. The workarounds are a foreign card or a paid intermediary (these are commercial sources) — [vc.ru](https://vc.ru/services/3083640-kak-oplatit-podpisku-canva-pro-iz-rossii); [DTF](https://dtf.ru/howto/4759740-oplata-canva-iz-rossii-v-2026-godu); [klerk.ru](https://www.klerk.ru/blogs/raketa-pay/706267/)

### Inferences
- PixReel's free, local, RU/EN tool with no account is likely to stand out for Russian-speaking users, since Canva, the best-known mass-market tool, is blocked there.

### Gaps
- Whether each product has a Russian UI (Canva, Clideo, FlexClip, Renderforest, Fotor, ezgif and Convertio are believed to offer RU or many languages): **unverified** in this session.
- Accessibility and payment from Russia for Kapwing, VEED, FlexClip, Clideo, Animoto and others: no sources found.

## Q6. Public API

### Takeaway
None of the searches turned up public rendering APIs for Kapwing, Clideo, Renderforest or Wave.video. The converters (Convertio, Online-Convert) are known to have APIs, but this was **not verified** here.

### Cited Findings
- The API Tracker pages for Kapwing mention SDKs but give no endpoint details. Nothing was found for Clideo, Renderforest or Wave.video — [apitracker.io](https://apitracker.io/a/kapwing/developers)
- img2go: one directory lists the API as "Not available yet" (unreliable) — [allinaipro](https://allinaipro.com/ai/img2go)
- Canva has an Apps SDK (for embedding apps inside Canva), not a rendering API for slideshows — [Canva dev docs](https://www.canva.dev/docs/apps/uploading-assets/)

### Inferences
- A public images→video rendering API with exotic inputs (HEIC, RAW, PSD, PDF, EXR) would face little direct competition from the slideshow makers. The likely competitors are general conversion APIs (Convertio, Online-Convert/api2convert, CloudConvert) and video-rendering APIs (Shotstack, Creatomate). None of these were researched in this session.

### Gaps
- Convertio API and api2convert (Online-Convert) pricing and limits: not researched or verified.
- Adobe Express (Embed SDK), Canva Connect API and Clipchamp API capabilities for programmatic video export: not verified.
